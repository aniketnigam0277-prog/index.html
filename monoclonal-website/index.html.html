01 · Basics

What is amonoclonal antibody?

  • Antibodies are proteins produced naturally by B cells.
  • A monoclonal antibody comes from a single selected clone.
  • All copies recognise the same epitope.
  • Antigen = the target molecule. Epitope = the exact site the antibody binds.
Polyclonal
Mixture from many B-cell clones; binds many epitopes.
Monoclonal
One clone, one epitope, uniform batches.

02 · Production

Hybridomatechnology

1 ImmunisationMouse receives the target antigen.
2 B-cell harvestingAntibody-producing B cells taken from spleen.
3 Fusion with myeloma cellsHybridomas form.
4 HAT selectionOnly hybridomas survive.
5 Screening and cloningELISA finds the right clones.
6 Production and purificationExpand the clone, harvest, purify.
Myeloma cells: immortality
Cancerous plasma cells that divide indefinitely. Fusion gives the hybridoma long-term growth.
HAT medium: selection
Aminopterin blocks the main nucleotide pathway. HGPRT-deficient myeloma cells die, unfused B cells die out, hybrids survive.
ELISA: screening purpose
Tests culture fluid to find which clones secrete antibody that binds the target antigen.
Limitation: mouse origin
Mouse antibodies can trigger a human anti-mouse immune response, which is why chimeric and humanized antibodies were engineered. Modern routes also include phage display, with large-scale production in CHO cells.
Sources: [1], [2] · First described by Köhler & Milstein, 1975 (Nobel Prize 1984).

04 · Action

Mechanismsof action

Neutralisation
Binds a virus or toxin and blocks entry or activity.
Example: palivizumab binds the RSV F protein.
Immune-cell recruitment
Fc region engages Fc receptors on NK cells and macrophages, and complement.
Example: rituximab (CD20) can trigger ADCC.
Receptor blocking · PD-1
PD-1 on T cells is a brake when it meets PD-L1 on tumour cells. Pembrolizumab binds PD-1 and blocks PD-L1/PD-L2 binding, so T cells can attack.
Antibody–drug conjugates
Antibody carries a cytotoxic drug to cells showing the target.
Example: trastuzumab emtansine → HER2-positive cells.
Sources: [2], [4]
05 · Applications

Where theyare used

Cancer · Trastuzumab
Targets HER2 on HER2-positive tumour cells.
Autoimmune disease · Adalimumab
Binds TNF-α, a pro-inflammatory cytokine (e.g. rheumatoid arthritis).
Infections · Palivizumab
Binds the RSV F protein; prevents severe RSV disease in high-risk infants.
Diagnostics · Pregnancy tests
Antibodies can detect hCG, a hormone produced during pregnancy.
Sources: [2], [4]
06 · Case study

Trastuzumabin practice

  • HER2-positive breast cancer: extra HER2 gene copies or too much HER2 protein drive growth signals.
  • Trastuzumab: a humanized antibody that binds the HER2 receptor.
  • Biomarker testing: HER2 status in the tumour decides who receives the drug.
Benefit
With chemotherapy in metastatic disease, median survival rose from 20.3 to 25.1 months in one randomised trial.
Limitation
Boxed warning for cardiomyopathy: heart function must be monitored.

07 · Quality

Quality controland testing

ELISA screening → culture → purification → release testing

Binding specificity
ELISA and other binding assays confirm the intended target is bound and not unrelated molecules.
Antibody concentration
Protein quantification (e.g. UV absorbance) so dose and assay amounts are accurate.
Purity and consistency
Chromatography and gel profiles compared between batches.
Contamination testing
Endotoxin, microbial and mycoplasma checks, and viral safety.
The exact tests depend on the product and manufacturing stage.

Quick quiz

Testyourself

Think first, then click a card to reveal the answer.

1. Rituximab: chimeric or humanized?
Chimeric (-ximab): mouse variable regions, human constant regions.
2. Why do unfused myeloma cells die in HAT medium?
They lack HGPRT, so with the main pathway blocked by aminopterin they cannot make nucleotides.
3. What does pembrolizumab block?
PD-1 binding to PD-L1/PD-L2, releasing the brake on T cells.
4. Which region binds the antigen: Fab or Fc?
Fab. The Fc region engages immune cells.

Questions?

Backup · Q&A

Possible questions

Why are monoclonal antibodies so expensive?
Complex cell-culture manufacturing, purification, strict quality testing and costly development and trials.
Why were mouse cells used first?
Mouse immunisation plus hybridoma fusion was the established route to a single antibody-producing clone; humanisation came later to reduce immunogenicity.
Do they have side effects?
Yes: infusion reactions, immunogenicity, and target-related effects such as the cardiac warning for trastuzumab.
Antigen vs epitope?
Antigen is the whole target molecule; the epitope is the exact site the antibody binds.
